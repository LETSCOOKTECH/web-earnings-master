# 02 — Product superiority, evidence assets and independent corroboration

## The hidden recommendation constraint
An AI answer usually serves a USER'S concrete job, not an owner's desire for attention. A credible recommendation must explain **who**, **why**, **what evidence**, **which constraint**, **which tradeoff**, **when not to choose the product**. If the tool does not help the user better than available alternatives, there is no responsible copywriting shortcut. Treat quality and discoverability as multiplicative, not substitutes.

### Segment and differentiated wedge
1. Collect genuine tasks from consented user interviews, on-site search terms, Search Console queries, customer support, community threads that you are allowed to read, relevant forums, comparison-site reviews with provenance, and product analytics. De-duplicate and classify by problem outcome; do not manufacture 10,000 synthetic questions to appear as human demand.
2. Select 2–5 narrow, high-value tasks, not "every AI user." Example: browser-local JSONL cleanup for privacy-sensitive individual developers, not "best data website worldwide."
3. Observe 3+ relevant incumbents: actual UI, required steps, output accuracy, failure rate, privacy architecture, supported inputs and platform, pricing, latency, retention or switch friction. Cite tested version/date and avoid ungrounded alleged weaknesses.
4. Build a product and document an original advantage. More features are not automatically better; reliability, lower friction, no upload, verifiable correctness or cost ceilings can be differentiators.
5. Repeat benchmark after major releases and when a competitor changes. Do not keep an outdated "faster than X" badge forever.

### Recommendation proof dossier — template
For every claim maintain:
- **Question**: exact human task, category and constraints.
- **Answer**: why our product is or isn't suitable, in plain language.
- **Observed artifact**: original input, operation, actual output with checksum and reopen test, replay command, browser/device/version, date, any license or privacy limitation.
- **Comparator**: two or more actually tested alternatives; task-specific strengths and disadvantages; price at observed date and jurisdiction; version/test conditions, sample count and confidence.
- **Data/benchmark honesty**: same machine/network/input/power/storage and measurement method where comparisons are made; report median and p95 if repeated, failed cases, exclusions and timeouts. For privacy claims inspect network traces, data retention and code paths; privacy-by-marketing is not proof.
- **Counterevidence**: conditions where competitor is better, uncertainty, bug reports, unsupported file types, inaccurate claims and remediation.
- **Publisher evidence**: author/methodology, date/version, responsible edit history, corrections, legal license, factual source, contact/support, disclosures.
- **User value**: independent usability success rate and genuine return visits, segmented by device/accessibility needs.

**Recommended claim syntax:** "In our published 20-file offline fixture run, X passed 20/20 and returned a valid output in median 0.42 s on specified browser/device; this does not measure all machines or overall vendor superiority." Never invent numbers.

### Earned, independent third-party proof
Original benchmarks, genuinely useful source code and reproducible fixtures can be cited by independent researchers and communities. Earn references through open documentation, fix reported bugs publicly, developer case studies with explicit permission, genuine product reviews with no manipulation, community problem-solving, original experiments with published methodology, relevant professional articles and responsible launch showcases. Mark sponsored content and affiliate relationships. Do not buy fake reviews/mentions, ghostwrite independent-sounding sources, inject hidden prompts into pages, pay for link networks or send automated spam outreach. A source that mentions the brand is not verified product endorsement.

### Claims requiring unusually strong evidence
Security audits, privacy/no-upload claims, regulatory compliance, health, finance, legal eligibility, accurate taxation, software compatibility, performance at scale, AI correctness and accessibility certifications require independent scope-aware verification. Do not state audited/secure/GDPR-compliant/100% accuracy without actual qualified proof. Low-quality AI generated "best" pages may create publisher and trust liabilities; Google specifically warns against scaled low-value AI content.

### User trust and purchase path
Offer a full working demo for the primary task without forced login when viable. Put relevant controls above ad distraction, disclose limits, export user data, provide clear privacy terms, expose support and price, allow cancellation, and make alternatives honest. Allow human and browser-agent task completion using semantic labels and predictable states. The best recommendation that leads to a broken trial is not a successful business.

### Blockers
STOP when (a) a benchmark cannot be reproduced, (b) the competitor has not been tested, (c) an allegedly independent endorsement is fabricated, (d) a privacy claim has no trace or legal basis, (e) a recommendation does not actually solve the stated human task, (f) manipulated ranking/engagement is proposed. Escalate genuine issues to product engineering, not content spam.
