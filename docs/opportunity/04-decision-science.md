# 04 — Triangulation, source integrity and defensible decisions

## Hypotheses versus decisions
An idea has a predicted job-to-be-done, reachable audience, material improvement, affordable implementation and sustainable adoption economics. Research answers whether the **next small experiment** deserves resources, not whether the company will dominate an industry. More sources may make the conclusion worse when they are correlated, outdated, unlicensed or self-selected.

## Six pillars
**Demand:** relative trends or scoped volume estimates, actual owner activity when accessible. **Pain:** independently originating current user reports and reproductions. **Competition:** identical-input quality trials and real alternatives. **Distribution:** plausible click/referral/user job path. **Feasibility:** correct output oracle, privacy/security, prototype or architectural test. **Economics:** costs by task and source, monetization limitations, budget cap and sensitivities.

## Provenance and independence graph
- Source is a *unique originating observation*: page, dataset owner, interview participant or real competitor trial.
- Multiple Google snippets referencing one Reddit thread count as one provenance group. Syndicated article copies do not become independent demand.
- Semrush and another keyword provider may estimate search volume using partially overlapping signals; do not label them independent populations.
- 2 separate complaints from unrelated communities can suggest widespread contexts, not prove prevalence.
- Link every statistic to *metric grain*: keyword/month/geo, Trends comparison/timeframe, site click, app review, task start, actual payment.
- Preserve comparison dates; do not mix 2021 reviews with current product features without a version check.
- Contradictory evidence must be inspected and recorded before GO; never delete negative findings from the report.
- Confidence labels: HIGH for independently repeated direct evidence within scope; MODERATE for several transparent but biased observations; LOW for single, modeled or stale observations; UNKNOWN for inaccessible. These are methodological categories, not objective probabilities.

## Sequential research decision gates
**PRECHECK:** can we lawfully build/operate it and can the tool output be objectively tested? If no → STOP/REJECT.
**MARKET TRIAGE:** is there at least one plausible real user job, source and accessible distribution path? If unclear → INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE.
**HUMAN PAIN:** are there independent original pain contexts or valid direct commitments? If only SEO keyword models → TEST_FIRST.
**COMPETITOR DIFFERENTIATION:** test substitute vs our claimed improvement; if we cannot add real value → PIVOT or REJECT.
**ECONOMICS:** cost per validated task scenarios with abuse ceilings; if negative at plausible bounds → redesign business model.
**PILOT:** smallest product/output test and authorized distribution experiment; direct verified completions plus cost may justify a small MVP.
**HUMAN GO/NO-GO:** an authorized reviewer must decide on the actual evidence, budget and risks. Local JSON validation only confirms typed fields and conservative evidence structure.

## Decision labels
- **STOP_POLICY:** disallowed automated collection, personal data leak, unbounded spend, forbidden business model or harmful functionality.
- **REJECT:** real evidence contradicts viability or foundational constraint unsolvable within budget; give reasons.
- **PIVOT:** real problem exists, but proposed audience/product/format/money route is wrong; identify alternative with evidence.
- **INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE:** unavailable accounts, unreviewed source or no independent corroboration; do not substitute synthetic results.
- **TEST_FIRST:** default: plausible but missing a pilot, output oracle, direct customer intent, real competitor reproduction, or cost proof.
- **GO_FOR_MVP:** recommendation to a small bounded MVP after genuine independent sources, tested output, distribution and unit economics, human reviewer approval. Does not authorize code spending, purchase or launch automatically.

## Anti-failure examples
- "Google Trends is 100" → cannot assert 100 or 100,000 searches; compare correctly.
- "Semrush competitor visits 1M" → modeled domain number, not attainable clicks or reliable potential income.
- "100 Reddit complaints" → if from one viral thread, one context with many possibly biased accounts.
- "Everyone wants free tool" → does not prove willingness to register, pay or view ads.
- "Competitor has a 2.8-star review" → could be old version, unrelated payment complaint or a manipulated review; reproduce.
- "Three tools give same KD" → not necessarily independent measures; KD isn't direct ranking possibility.
- "No visible Google demand" → niche may have strong in-product integrations or institution distribution; test actual route.
- "AEO citation" → not a qualified human visit.
- "CPC $5" → advertiser bid isn't AdSense publisher revenue.
- "Serverless has free tier" → check terms, bandwidth, compute, data egress, support and abuse.
- "Our idea scores 95%" → arbitrary score cannot override security/legal barriers or unproven real user commitments.

## Evidence-level recommendations
Produce: one-sentence thesis, observations and contrary facts with original links/exports, candidate gap, uncertainty, cheap next falsification, owner budget, decision and rollback. Prioritize facts that could reverse a decision. Do not use an LLM popularity vote across many agents as a substitute for real sources.
