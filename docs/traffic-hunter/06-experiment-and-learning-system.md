# 06 — Pilot experiments, systematic learning and channel allocation

## The unit of experimentation
One **working task × one user cohort × one distribution channel × one distinct value asset × one target landing page × one time window**. Without this grain, marketing teams incorrectly credit a batch of uncontrolled changes for movement in search or AI citations.

## Pre-registration
- Name job and whether first-time/repeat intent is expected.
- Specify country/language/device and launch venue account (not credentials).
- Classify permission (first-party policy opened, uncertain, prohibited).
- Link actual verified product output evidence, current privacy/AdSense policy gate.
- Define sample window or observation rule without guaranteed volume.
- Primary metric: **validated task completion from legitimate landing sessions**. If only referral sessions can be observed, say so.
- Secondary: referrer sessions, starts, completion rate, voluntary returns, earned editorial citations, related-tool completions, opted-in signups, paid conversion when relevant.
- Cost: asset production hours, campaign spend, variable compute/provider cost, support/moderation overhead, net real revenue when observed.
- Guardrails: suspicious or unqualified traffic, privacy leak, user harm, bad output, ad policy signals, false claims, rate-limits, unsolicited outreach, community rejections.
- Counterhypothesis: audience not present, CTA cannot be followed, competitor dominates, product UX fails, visit source lost, short-term seasonality, paid traffic low-margin.
- Decision and accountable operator with actual authorization.

## Prioritization and balancing exploration vs exploitation
1. Keep two pilot slots on high-fit but still unproven channels; one slot may validate a speculative but low-risk discovery mechanism.
2. Compare **qualified successes and cost** rather than raw impressions. Platform views can be useful to diagnose reach but do not belong in an ad revenue numerator.
3. Stop immediately for critical policy or privacy risk; no statistical threshold is needed for safety.
4. If a low-cost pilot produces useful feedback but insufficient traffic, improve the product asset and run a bounded second test.
5. If profitable repeatable channel seems strong, expand only after another cohort/period and actual capacity/consent review; do not declare a causal lift if several events overlapped.
6. Preserve a decision log: selection, precise inputs, action approval, observed outcome, limitations, rollback and what to test next.
7. Domain growth should target real jobs/users and maintain source diversification—not buy 'backup traffic' from unreliable resellers.

## Operating cadence (a plan, not an autonomous background service)
**Daily when manually invoked or legitimately scheduled by an authorized system:** review current public task signals and actual account anomalies, not automate platform posting. Escalate quality/policy incidents.
**Weekly:** compare top 2–3 executed pilots, qualified sessions, valid task output, direct user feedback, campaign expenses and unresolved acquisition observations; stop weak channels.
**Monthly:** reassess markets, locale variation, brand/referral activity, unit economics, real product upgrades and portfolio capital allocation.
**Quarterly:** reconsider dependencies on one platform, owned audience depth, vendor changes, SEO/AI reporting changes and direct returning usage; re-run source-truth checks.

## Growth experiments that can be measured
A. URL discoverability: after a repaired internal link/sitemap issue, monitor verified GSC discovery/indexing and task visits, rather than claiming ranking.
B. Video CTA: truthful clearer demonstration versus vague link, compare platform outbound (where available), owned sessions and task success. No bought views.
C. Community question: answer a representative technical task comprehensively without link; track direct independent references or permitted relevant link afterward.
D. Partnership: one accurately targeted library/teacher guide, observe tasks and any real long-run referral, no demanded backlinks.
E. Product handoff: allow validated output directly into one related adjacent tool; measure additional completion and consent.
F. Localization: quality-checked true locale requirement vs generic English page; task correctness and local referral, no doorway translations.
G. Paid keyword pilot: fix cap and geo; require user starts and actual success, separate revenue not assumed; no ad-funded click arbitrage.
H. Open integration: release one maintained SDK and document tasks; adoption can be valuable even if no website sessions.

## Decision states
- `RESEARCH`: plausible signals but unverified policy or product.
- `READY_FOR_HUMAN_REVIEW`: scored opportunity, current primary source, truthful asset, costs and measurement, no red flags.
- `AUTHORIZED_EXPERIMENT`: owner approved specific launch action and bounded risk (the evaluator never grants this state itself).
- `OBSERVING`: external action actually happened and dated metrics are being collected.
- `EXPAND`: repeated evidence shows useful jobs, tolerable cost and policy safety.
- `ITERATE`: limited task outcome with identifiable remediable failure.
- `PAUSE`: economics or attribution insufficient, unclear audience or exhausted budget.
- `STOP`: policy/security/invalid traffic/deception/user harm.

## Counterexamples
- 10,000 short views with no attributable tool success is not a successful traffic campaign.
- A new editorial backlink coinciding with an update does not prove it caused ranking gain.
- 100 crawler requests cannot be counted as 100 qualified visitors.
- A social post that solves the problem in-platform may do useful work without site visits; avoid maximizing clicks at expense of user's need.
- A source can underreport due to attribution/privacy; label uncertainty, do not fabricate missing conversions.
